        How backfields are tiered

        Usage is each running back's share of his team's RB carries plus targets, counted only in games he played, so a missed game doesn't drag his number down.

        • Every Down: a back at 69% or more, either for the season or over the last 4 games.
        • Committee: three backs at 15% or more, with nobody at 50%.
        • Split: everything else, usually a lead back plus a real second option.
        • Backs on IR or PUP come off the depth chart, so the next backs move up and the tier is based on who can actually play. They're listed under Out on the team card.

        The tier measures workload, not production. The RB1 and RB2 badges answer whether he's worth starting.

        What makes a true handcuff

        A handcuff is the next healthy back behind an every-down starter. If the starter goes down, he inherits a workload no one else on the roster is sharing. The #2 back in a split already has a role, so his value doesn't depend on an injury.

        Handcuffs are ranked by the starter's share: the more of the backfield the starter owns, the bigger the job waiting for his backup.
